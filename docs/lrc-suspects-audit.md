The question: is a suspect `LYRICS` tag bad because lrclib supplied bad input, because our own tools broke it, or can it not be told? Each track is compared against three sources: the tag, lrclib today, and a demucs + whisperx transcript of the audio (the `lrc_fix` ASR cache). The verdicts are heuristics, not proof; the rules are at the end.

## Rules and thresholds

- `no_audio_evidence`: under 5 transcript words, or under 10 words per minute.
- `instrumental_has_lyrics` (checked first): `LYRICS_LANGUAGE` is `zxx` and whisper hears under 10 words per minute, or the track has no real language tag (none, `und`, `zxx`) and whisper hears under 3. A track MusicBrainz calls sung is never one, whatever whisper hears.
- Is the text sung at all? Sung ratio = share of the lyric's consecutive word pairs that occur anywhere in the transcript, independent of order, so repeats, a shuffled line order and backing vocals do not matter: under 20% `text_mismatch`, under 50% `text_partial`. 25 tracks lie within 5% of a threshold (see the distribution below).
- The tag only ends early (no stacked timestamps) and the text is sung: the tag's own timestamps are compared with where whisper hears the same lines. A median distance of 3 s or less over at least 60% of the lines is `ends_early_confirmed` (the lyric is simply shorter than the song: repeats and outro are not written out), or `ends_early_borderline` if the tag ends at 45% of the track or later; a larger distance is `timestamps_shifted`; too few matching lines is `ends_early_unconfirmed`. Hearts only is `source_junk_only`.
- Stacked timestamps and fewer than 60% of the word-bearing lines matching in order (the quality gate's `--min-match`): `text_ok_not_alignable`. The words are there, but `lrc_fix` still cannot place enough lines in order (ad-libs, or a transcript with hallucinated loops).
- Stacked timestamps and alignable: lrclib decides. No synced lyrics there and the tag was written by `lrc_fix` is `stacked_by_lrc_fix` (its own earlier output; lrclib never had stacked ones), no synced lyrics and another writer is `timestamps_bad_source_unknown`; lrclib stacked too is `lrclib_bad_timestamps` (identical timestamps) or `lrclib_bad_other_version`; lrclib fine means our tools wrote it (`tag_worse_than_lrclib_lrc_fix` if the tag has a `lrc_fix` `[re:]` header, otherwise `..._other`, which also covers an older lrclib version).
- "Would fix" is a simulation, nothing is written: today's `lrc_fix` (line match, sung ratio and no stacked result) would write the tag from the cached transcript. "Would reject" means it would leave the tag as it is.
- A "safe bet" is a track with stacked timestamps, a sung ratio of at least 50%, "would fix", cached words and onsets (so no ML is needed) and a tag unchanged since `prepare`. `lrc_audit.py fix --apply` repairs exactly these.

## Caveats

- whisper `medium` can miss quiet, heavily processed or non-English vocals; those land in `no_audio_evidence`/`text_mismatch` although the lyrics may be right.
- The tags are audited as they were when `prepare` ran; lrclib data is as of the fetch date (cached in the work directory, `--refetch` renews it).
- `[re:]` only says who wrote the tag last; a manual edit or an older lrclib version looks the same as `update_lyrics` output.
- Per-track results: `lrc_suspects_audit.csv`/`.xlsx` in the repository root (git-ignored).
